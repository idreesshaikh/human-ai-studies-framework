# Participant information sheet

**Study:** AI assistance and developer cognitive load: a within-subjects
pilot (`pilot-2026`)
**Researchers:** Idrees Razak (facilitator) · Project Supervisor
**Contact:** razakidrees@gmail.com
**Status:** DRAFT - not for use until approved with the ethics application.

## What is this study?

We are studying how working with an AI coding assistant changes the
experience of programming - mental effort, moments of being stuck, and the
code that results. You will do two short programming sessions: once with an
AI assistant (Claude Code) available, once without.

## What will I do?

- Two sessions of **45 minutes** each (plus ~10 minutes of briefing and a
  short end-of-session questionnaire), on separate occasions or with a
  break between.
- In each session you fix two bugs and add one small feature to a small
  Python program we provide, inside VS Code on the researcher's laptop.
  A test suite tells you when you're done; finishing everything is **not**
  expected.
- Occasionally a small in-editor prompt asks how tired you feel (one
  keypress, 1-5); at the end a short workload questionnaire appears.

## What data is collected?

We designed the instruments to record **how** you work, never your words or
your code as text:

- Self-reports: your fatigue ratings, "stuck" responses, and the end
  questionnaire.
- Editing behavior: timing, sizes, and origin of edits (typed / pasted /
  AI-inserted), which parts of files were visible, paste **sizes** - never
  the pasted text, never keystrokes, never clipboard content.
- Code measurements: automatic complexity metrics computed from the task
  code you produce, and snapshots of that task code (it is our exercise
  repository, not your personal code).
- AI session (AI condition only): **timing and size** of your messages to
  and from the assistant and which tools it used - **not** the text of the
  conversation.

You are identified in all data only as an anonymous ID (e.g. "P03"). The
sheet linking your name to that ID stays on paper with the researcher and
is destroyed at study end. All study data stays on the researcher's
machine; nothing is uploaded to any cloud service.

## Your rights

Participation is voluntary. You may pause or stop a session at any time
without giving a reason, and you may withdraw your data at any point up to
analysis (contact us with your participant ID); withdrawal has no
consequences. This study has been approved through the University of
Manchester Department of Computer Science ethics process (reference: see
consent form).
